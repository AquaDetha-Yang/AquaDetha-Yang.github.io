HMI Design · 2025

L3 Autonomous
Driving System

A human-centered HMI design for Level 3 autonomous driving — where the vehicle handles dynamic driving tasks, but the driver must be ready to take over at any moment. The design challenge lies in the handover.

Overview

The challenge of
shared control.

L3

Conditional Automation

At Level 3, the driver becomes a fallback — not continuously monitoring, but ready to intervene within seconds. The interface must maintain trust, communicate system confidence, and make the handover seamless.

User Journey

From manual to
autonomous and back.

Phase 01

Manual Departure

Driver navigates out of the parking structure and through urban streets manually. The system monitors conditions in the background, preparing for autonomy availability on the highway segment ahead.

Autonomous Activation

As the vehicle enters the highway, the system signals readiness. Driver initiates L3 mode with a deliberate physical input. The steering wheel retracts slightly. Ambient lighting shifts to a calm blue spectrum — the vehicle now drives itself.

Phase 03

Eyes-Free Engagement

During sustained autonomy, the driver engages in non-driving tasks — reading, messaging, or simply resting. The HMI maintains peripheral awareness through subtle ambient cues, indicating system confidence through color temperature and gentle rhythmic pulses.

Phase 04

Takeover Request

As the highway exit approaches or weather degrades, the system initiates a staged takeover: early advisory → soft alert → escalating urgency. Multi-modal feedback (visual, haptic, audible) guides the driver back to the loop with calm precision, not panic.

Manual Resumption

Driver confirms takeover with steering input. The system transitions back cleanly — wheel extends, ambient light returns to standard white, dashboard reconfigures to manual driving layout. The handover is complete.

Key Considerations

What matters most
at Level 3.

01

Mode
Confusion

Drivers must never be uncertain about who is in control. Clear, redundant mode indicators — across instrument cluster, HUD, and ambient lighting — eliminate ambiguity at every moment.

02

Takeover
Timing

L3 requires a takeover window of 8–15 seconds. The interface must provide progressive urgency without inducing panic. Timing, escalation curves, and feedback modality are critical design parameters.

03

Trust
Calibration

Over-trust leads to complacency; under-trust defeats the purpose of autonomy. The HMI must display system confidence transparently — showing what the vehicle sees, what it plans, and when it's uncertain.

Design Considerations

How we translate
requirements into
experience.

Ambient Elements:
Strategic Retreat

In autonomous mode, ambient elements — lighting, sound, haptic feedback — deliberately recede to create a calm, low-stimulation environment. This "strategic retreat" reduces cognitive load and allows the driver to disengage from continuous monitoring. When the system needs attention, these elements gradually re-engage, creating a natural rhythm between engagement and relaxation.

Autonomous Driving Elements:
Progressive Evolution

As autonomy matures, the driving interface evolves from explicit status indicators to implicit environmental cues. Early phases rely on prominent icons and alerts; advanced phases use subtle spatial audio, peripheral lighting shifts, and predictive path visualization. The UI adapts to the driver's growing familiarity, reducing visual clutter while maintaining safety-critical awareness.

Backstage State

The system continuously monitors driver readiness, environmental conditions, and system health — all operating in the background without demanding visual attention. This "backstage state" architecture ensures that critical information surfaces only when necessary, while non-essential data remains accessible but unobtrusive. The driver experiences seamless autonomy while the system maintains vigilance.

Design Showcase

Visuals, motion,
and details.

L3 Full Demo

Falcon Mode — a dedicated third-person perspective designed specifically for L3 autonomous driving. It visually communicates the driver's transition from active controller to passive supervisor, displaying the vehicle's spatial awareness, planned path, and system confidence from an elevated bird's-eye view. This unique perspective reinforces the psychological shift required at Level 3: you are no longer driving — you are overseeing.

Narrow Path Perspective

When navigating narrow roads or tight passages, the camera automatically elevates to a non-perspective bird's-eye view. This overhead angle provides clear spatial awareness of road width and surrounding obstacles, helping the driver understand the vehicle's exact position relative to narrow gaps — essential for confident supervision in constrained environments.

MRM — Minimal Risk Maneuver

When the system encounters an edge case it cannot handle — or when the driver fails to respond to takeover requests — it initiates a Minimal Risk Maneuver. The vehicle safely pulls over to the roadside, activates hazard lights, and comes to a controlled stop. The interface clearly communicates the MRM state, reasoning, and final position to the driver.
