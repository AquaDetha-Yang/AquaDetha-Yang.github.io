Display Surfaces · One System

NIO OS 2.0 was born from a simple question: why does every screen in the car feel like it was designed by a different team? Our answer was to define a unified design language, interaction model, and information architecture across all cockpit surfaces — making the vehicle feel like a single, intelligent space.

User Journey

Mapped across
every moment.

Continuous Companions

Navigation, media apps accompany the user throughout the entire journey — always present, always accessible, adapting to context but never disappearing.

Intermittent Engagement

Car control functions — climate, seat adjustments, window controls — appear sporadically as needed. They surface when relevant and recede when not, respecting the user's primary focus on driving.

Conditional Automation

Pilot assistance, constrained by current performance limitations, cannot sustain full-journey autonomous driving. It activates only on road segments where conditions meet its operational design domain — providing partial relief, not complete liberation.

Center Display · Information Architecture

The center stack follows a three-layer architecture: persistent global controls at the bottom, a main content canvas in the center, and contextual shortcuts in a collapsible sidebar. This structure ensures muscle memory forms quickly — critical functions are always in the same place, regardless of which app or view is active.

Instrument Cluster · Information Priority

The cluster follows a three-layer hierarchy: Layer 1 (NIO Pilot) occupies the center as the primary visual focus. Layer 2 flanks it with essential driving data — speed, power, and scene information on left and right. Layer 3 (ambient — time, temperature, system mode) lives at the edges. This structure ensures critical information is always prominent while contextual data remains accessible.

Market & Vision

Where we are,
where we're going.

Current Landscape

Chinese OEMs lead in cockpit OS innovation — NIO, Xpeng, Li Auto, and Huawei have each invested heavily in vertically integrated software stacks, outpacing traditional automakers in UI sophistication and update frequency.

The gap between premium EV cockpits and legacy ICE interfaces has become a chasm. Consumers now evaluate cockpit experience as a primary purchase criterion — on par with range and charging infrastructure.

Voice + touch + gesture multimodal interaction is the new baseline. Systems that rely on touch alone feel dated within a single product cycle. The best cockpits adapt input modality to driving context — voice while moving, touch while parked.

Key Challenges

Screen proliferation without information architecture leads to overwhelm. More displays ≠ better experience. The hardest design problem is deciding what not to show.

Hardware-software integration is the real bottleneck. Sensors, displays, lighting, and audio must sync perfectly — each with different latencies and update cycles. Seamless orchestration requires deep cross-disciplinary collaboration.

Mobility Agent learns user behaviors and preferences over time, building a persistent memory that makes the cockpit genuinely personal. It anticipates needs, adapts routines, and transforms the space into the user's own environment.
